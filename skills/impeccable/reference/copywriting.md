# Copywriting — Persuasive Page Copy

> Adapted from coreyhaines31/marketingskills @ 4b377f2 (MIT). Full ledger: ../NOTICE.md

**Scope:** labels, errors, empty states, and microcopy belong to [ux-writing](ux-writing.md). THIS file owns the words that sell — headlines, subheads, CTAs, and body copy on marketing surfaces.

## The Real-Content Rule (NORMATIVE)

Write the actual copy from the brief BEFORE building layout. Real copy drives structure: a 4-word H1 and a 12-word H1 want different heroes; three real benefits kill the fourth filler card.

- DO NOT ship lorem ipsum. Ever.
- DO NOT ship `[placeholder]`, `[Your headline here]`, `TK`, or "Insert testimonial".
- Placeholder text found at audit = build failure (audit-rules.md, group F).
- Missing facts? Ask, or write honest copy without them. NEVER fabricate stats, customer counts, or testimonials.

## Research Before Writing

1. **Context check:** read `.impeccable.md` Design Context, plus `.claude/product-marketing.md` if present. Only ask for gaps.
2. **Page purpose:** the ONE primary action this page exists to produce.
3. **Audience:** the problem they're solving, their objections, and the *exact language they use* — mirror voice-of-customer from reviews, interviews, support tickets.
4. **Offer:** differentiator vs alternatives, the key transformation, real proof points.
5. **Traffic:** where visitors arrive from and what they already know. A landing headline must match the ad they clicked.

Stance while writing: clarity over cleverness. Benefits over features. Specificity over vagueness ("Cut weekly reporting from 4 hours to 15 minutes", not "Save time"). Customer language over company language. One idea per section.

## Page-Type Templates

**Homepage** — outcome-first H1, ≤9 words. Subhead states the *mechanism* (how the outcome is possible, 1–2 sentences). ONE primary CTA above the fold. Serve multiple audiences with the broadest value prop, then clear paths per intent — never go generic to please everyone.

**Landing page** — single message, single CTA, headline matches the traffic source. Complete the argument on one page: hero → proof bar → problem → how it works (3 steps) → 2–3 benefits → testimonial → FAQ → final CTA + risk reversal. NOT Hero → Feature 1 → Feature 2 → Feature 3 → CTA — that's a list, not an argument.

Variants: **compact** (ad traffic) — hero → proof bar → 3 benefits → testimonial → how it works → final CTA + guarantee. **Enterprise/B2B** — hero → logo bar → business-pain problem → solution overview → use cases by role → security/compliance → case study with metrics → demo CTA. **Launch** — announcement hero → demo video → 3–5 highlights → before/after → early testimonials → launch pricing + urgency CTA.

**Pricing page** — the copy's job is answering "which one is right for me?" Name who each plan is for. Make the recommended plan the obvious default. Put objections in the FAQ, not under a rug.

**About page** — the story of why you exist, connected to what the customer gets out of that. Still ends in a CTA.

**Feature page** — feature → benefit → outcome chain, in that order. Show use cases. Clear path to try or buy.

### H1 Formulas (pick by argument shape, not mood)

- `{Outcome} without {pain}` — "Understand how users experience your site without drowning in numbers"
- `Turn {input} into {outcome}` — "Turn your hard-earned sales into repeat customers"
- `{Outcome} in {timeframe}` — "Get your tax refund in 10 days"
- `Never {unpleasant event} again` — "Never miss a sales opportunity again"
- `Stop {pain}. Start {pleasure}.` — "Stop chasing invoices. Start getting paid on time."
- `{Question naming the pain}` — "Hate returning stuff to Amazon?"
- `{Category} for {audience}` — "Advanced analytics for Shopify e-commerce"
- `The {category} that {differentiator}` — "The CRM that updates itself"
- `The {opposite of the usual process} way to {outcome}` — "The easiest way to turn your passion into income"
- `{Number} {people} use {product} to {outcome}` — "50,000 marketers use Drip to send better emails"
- `Finally, {category} that {benefit}` — "Finally, accounting software that doesn't suck"
- `You don't have to {skill/resource} to {outcome}` — "You don't have to be an SEO pro to rank higher"

### Section Micro-Formulas

- **Problem section openers:** "You know the feeling…" / "If you're like most {role}…" / "Every day, {audience} struggles with…" — then name the specific frustration, the time or money wasted, and the impact.
- **Benefit block:** headline = the outcome. Body = how, in 1–2 sentences. Proof = a number or quote, when you have one.
- **How-it-works step:** number + simple verb + outcome — "1. Connect your tools (takes 2 minutes)".
- **Testimonials:** specific result ("increased conversions by 32%"), before/after context, role + company. Reject "Great product!", "Love it!", "Easy to use!".
- **Final CTA section:** recap the value, repeat the CTA, reverse the risk.

## CTA Formulas

`[Action verb] + [what they get] + [qualifier if needed]`

- Weak — DO NOT use: Submit, Sign Up, Learn More, Get Started, Click Here.
- Strong: "Start Free Trial", "See {Product} in Action", "Create Your First {Thing}", "Get the Complete Checklist", "See Pricing for My Team".
- Write the CTA in the customer's words for the thing they want, not your words for the thing you want.
- De-risk within sight of every CTA: guarantee, "No credit card required", "Cancel anytime", or what-happens-next.

## One Voice: Fonts and Copy

Reuse the SAME 3 concrete brand words you wrote in SKILL.md's font-selection procedure (Step 1). The typeface and the headline must speak with one voice — a font chosen for "warm and mechanical and opinionated" cannot sit above copy that reads "calm and clinical and careful." Test every headline against the 3 words before keeping it.

Drift checks (read the page aloud): starting casual then turning corporate; mixing "we" and "the company"; random spikes of technical language. Headlines bolder, body clearer, CTAs action-oriented — same voice, adjusted intensity. No exclamation points.

AI-tell transitions — banned: "That being said", "It's worth noting that", "At its core", "In today's digital landscape", "When it comes to the realm of", "This begs the question", "Let's delve into". Never open a paragraph with "In conclusion".

## The Seven Sweeps

Sequential editing passes over the finished draft. After each sweep, re-verify the earlier ones.

1. **Clarity** — every sentence understandable, clear pronoun references, no buried points. Then confirm one idea per section, and that the copy speaks to the reader ("you"), not about the company.
2. **Voice** — consistent formality and personality throughout. Read aloud.
3. **So What** — every claim answers "why should I care?" Add "which means…" bridges. "Our platform uses AI-powered analytics" → "…surfaces insights you'd miss manually, so you make better decisions in half the time."
4. **Prove It** — every claim backed: named testimonials, stats with sources, logos, guarantees. Flag "Trusted by thousands" (which thousands?) and "Industry-leading" (says who?). Add proof or soften the claim.
5. **Specificity** — "Save time" → "Save 4 hours every week". "Many customers" → "2,847 teams". "Fast results" → "Results in 14 days". If a claim can't be made specific, it's filler — delete it.
6. **Emotion** — paint the before-state vividly; sensory language; micro-stories. Emotion serves the message, never manipulation.
7. **Zero Risk** — list every reason to hesitate near each CTA; address each one; add risk reversals and trust signals.

### Cut on sight

very, really, extremely, incredibly · just, actually, basically · "in order to" → "to" · unnecessary "that" · things, stuff · a total of, as a matter of fact, at the end of the day, at this moment in time, in due course, it should be understood, last but not least, obviously, of course, quite, the fact of the matter is, to all intents and purposes.

### Replace on sight

| Weak | Strong |
|------|--------|
| Utilize | Use |
| Implement | Set up |
| Leverage | Use |
| Facilitate | Help |
| Innovative | New |
| Robust | Strong |
| Seamless | Smooth |
| Cutting-edge | New / Modern |
| Commence | Start |
| Prior to | Before |
| Due to the fact that | Because |
| Demonstrate | Show |

Also: kill adverbs, convert passive to active ("We generate reports", not "Reports are generated"), de-nominalize ("make a decision" → "decide").

### Sentence and paragraph mechanics

- One idea per sentence. Front-load the key information. Usually ≤25 words, ≤3 conjunctions. Vary length so the page has pulse.
- One topic per paragraph, 2–4 sentences for web. Strong openers. White space is a copywriting tool — break for scanning.
- Cut confident-sounding qualifiers: "almost", "very", "really". State the thing or don't.

### Anti-weasel ban list

These words mean nothing and mark the copy as generated. Banned in all page copy: seamless · empower · unlock · elevate · supercharge · effortless · revolutionize · game-changing · next-level · streamline · harness · transformative · cutting-edge · best-in-class · turbocharge · frictionless. Replace each with the specific thing the product actually does.
